In this video I introduce the new One ROM Family members. There's now a complete lineup of 24, 28, 32 (8-bit) and 40 pin (16-bit) versions of One ROM, and all the hardware designs you need to have your own One ROMs manufactured are open source, as is all of the firmware and software tooling. If you'd rather buy One ROMs, checkout <https://onerom.org/buy>.  

If you'd like to see another Youtuber's experience with One ROM, check out the latest Adrian's Digital Basement video. Adrian gets an IBM PC XT booting to BASIC in a crazy fast time, with a little help from One ROM: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Nscn9gBMJQ>  

One ROM can be used in a wide range of retro systems, including the Commodore PET, VIC-20, C64, 128, Amiga A500, BBC Micro and Master, Acorn Electron, Atari ST, Atari 8-bit machines, Apple II, IBM PCs 5150 and XT.  But the list is likely to be much, much longer.  

In terms of ROM types emulated, these include the 2316, 2332, 2364, 23128, 23256, 23512 and 231024 mask programmed ROMs (with any factory programmed chip select behaviour), and 2716, 2732, 2764, 27128, 27256, 27512, 271024, 27C010, 27C020, 27C040, 27C301 and 27C400 EPROMs.  Again, the list is likely to be much longer in practice, as manufacturers often had their own custom part numbers (for example, the 9316 used in early arcade machines, pinball machines and the Apple II is effectively a 2316).  

If you'd like to know whether your system or favourite ROM type is supported, or if you have any questions about One ROM, please get in touch in the comments, at GitHub discussions, or via email (it's not hard to find).
