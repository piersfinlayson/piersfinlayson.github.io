In this video I get my new One ROM USB boards working.  

One ROM aims to be the most flexible retro ROM replacement, replacing 24 pin ROMS like the 2364, 2332 and 2316 using a $2 microcontroller.  

Up until now you've needed to use another MCU (for example a $5 Raspberry Pi Pico) to program One ROM.  Now, you don't need anything other than One ROM USB, a USB cable, and a PC.  

I go through the design, firmware modification, troubleshooting and fix process - and give a sneak preview of an upcoming website which programs your USB One ROMs locally.
